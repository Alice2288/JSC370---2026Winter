Headline figures (HW5)

Three interactive visualisations covering dropdown filtering, time-axis animation, and 3D rotation.

1. Engagement and ratings

Figure 1. Vote average vs log10(vote count). Use the dropdown at the top-left to filter by primary genre, or keep "All genres" to compare across the eight most common ones. Films with more votes cluster in a narrower rating band, which is the empirical version of the idea that big audiences stabilise the average rating.

2. Films by decade — animated bubble chart

Figure 2. Each frame shows the films released in one decade, sized by popularity and coloured by primary genre. Click Play or drag the slider to step through time. Newer decades carry more films and a wider tail of vote counts; the rating distribution itself stays fairly stable, suggesting most of the change in TMDb's catalogue is in volume and visibility rather than in audience taste.

3. Budget × revenue × rating in 3D

Figure 3. Drag inside the plot to rotate the camera. Each axis is one log-scale variable: budget, revenue, and rating. The diagonal sheet of points is the standard more-budget-makes-more-revenue relationship; what the third dimension shows is that rating doesn't lie along that axis. High-rated and low-rated films can sit side-by-side at the same budget and revenue level.

Supplementary figures

Additional diagnostics referenced in the report.

4. ROC curves — high-rating classifier

Figure 4. ROC curves for the binary task of identifying films with vote_average ≥ 7.0. Each curve traces the true-positive rate against the false-positive rate as the decision threshold moves; the dashed grey line is chance. The Random Forest and XGBoost curves are essentially tied (AUC ≈ 0.87), both well above the logistic baseline. AUC values are in the legend.

5. Decade and genre composition

Figure 5. Sunburst chart with decade on the inner ring and primary genre on the outer ring. Slice size is the number of films and slice colour is the mean rating in that bucket. Click any slice to zoom in; click the centre to zoom out. Documentaries and animation reliably sit on the higher-rating end of the colour scale across decades; horror sits on the lower end.

6. Rating distributions by genre

Figure 6. Violin + box plot of vote averages, broken out by primary genre. Hover any violin to see its quartiles. Median ratings differ across genres but the within-genre spread is wide and overlaps a lot, which is why genre alone is a weak predictor of rating in the regression model.

7. Top features driving predicted rating

Figure 7. Top 20 features ranked by relative importance in the XGBoost regression model. Genre indicators (genre_animation, genre_drama, genre_music) lead the ranking, followed by is_english and release_year. Engagement variables (log_vote_count, log_popularity) sit in the middle of the ranking — informative but not dominant on this 1,500-film sample.

8. Predicted vs actual ratings

Figure 8. Predicted vs actual ratings on the held-out test set, faceted by model. The dashed line is perfect prediction. Linear regression visibly shrinks predictions toward the mean. Random Forest does better. XGBoost has the tightest scatter and the lowest RMSE. All three models slightly underpredict the very top of the rating scale, which is hard to fit with so few training examples up there.
